Checklist
1) RS >= +15  2) fresh breakout today  3) volume confirmed  4) healthy move (+1..8%, not extended)  5) not lagging the market  6) mainstream EQ series.
Tier A
5+ checks including strong RS AND a fresh breakout - today's highest-conviction setups.
Tier B
3-4 checks - worth a look, one or two things missing.
Tier C
0-2 checks - on the sheet, but weak by this playbook. Off by default in the Tier filter to keep the list short.

Wyckoff phases & the Setup filter

This tool finds stocks already leading and breaking out, which maps onto the later half of a Wyckoff cycle (Phases D-E: the breakout and markup) - not the early accumulation Spring. Each stock's phase is inferred from which screeners it fired:

SOS - Sign of Strength
A range/resistance breakout (Sideways-breakout, Range-breakout-with-volume, Master-resistance, Darvas, 52-week) firing today, on volume, while leading the market and not yet extended. Wyckoff's "jumping the creek" - the actionable breakout entry.
LPS - Last Point of Support
A leader (positive RS) resting quietly at support (Major-support or the 0.7-0.88 Fib zone) on a flat/down day - i.e. a low-volume backup after a prior breakout. The safer, lower-chase Wyckoff entry.
Base - cause building
Volatility contracting (Daily-contraction or VCP) with no breakout yet - the range is still forming. A watch-list, not an entry.
Setup filter: Any / Wyckoff SOS / Wyckoff LPS
One click. SOS also sets the companion filters (require fresh + volume, hide extended/laggards, RS >= +5). LPS reconfigures them for a quiet backup (fresh & volume OFF, RS >= +5, shows Tier C too, since a resting stock may score lower). You can still fine-tune afterward.
The manual step Wyckoff still needs
No filter confirms a phase - on the chart, verify a real prior trading range (the "cause"), light volume on pullbacks/Spring vs. expanding volume on the breakout, and that the pullback holds above the breakout level.

Episodic Pivot (the Setup filter's 4th option)

A stockbee-style catalyst move: the stock gaps up >= 4% on >= 3x normal volume - usually earnings or news igniting it out of a quiet base. Unlike relative strength (which measures "is it already strong"), this measures "did something just happen today," so it's genuinely different information. We tested it: standalone, EP names hit +20% about 2.5x more often than random; and within the RS-top-10, the ones that also just had an EP hit target ~24% vs ~17% for the rest. Clicking the preset shows gap-ups on volume and - unlike the other lenses - leaves "Extended >15%" OFF, because a big gap is the whole point.

Follow-through (why some gaps don't get the badge)
We backtested a refinement: an EP that closes in the top half of its day's range (it HELD the gap) hit target ~19%, versus only ~12% for one that faded to the bottom half - a +7pp edge. So the EP^ badge now fires only for gaps that held; a gap that faded is not flagged as actionable. Hold quality is the tell that the catalyst has real buyers behind it.
Reading it
Best when the gap comes off a tight base (check the chart) rather than after the stock has already run for weeks. A gap on the 1st day of a move > a gap on day 20.

Persistence & leadership (the 20-day strip)

A single day tells you a stock is strong today; the strip tells you whether it has stayed strong. We tested this: among the daily top-10 by relative strength, the ones that had led on 15+ of the prior 20 sheets went on to hit target ~24% of the time, versus ~15% for names that had only just emerged. Persistence is a real, second-order conviction signal - so it's shown as context and as cohort filters, but deliberately NOT folded into the score (relative strength already ranks these names to the top; persistence is how you choose between them).

Persistent leader
Led (RS >= +15) on 12+ of the last 20 sheets - a durable trend you can lean on. The higher-win cohort.
New leader (NEW*)
First day in the leader cohort - earlier, less confirmed, but a fresh idea before it's obvious.
Reading it with price
Persistence is only bullish alongside strength. A stock that's persistently on the list but NOT leading (a faint, breakout-less strip) is dead money, not a base - the strip makes that visible.

Emerging (catching a move early)

The persistence cohorts catch stocks that are ALREADY strong. Emerging tries to catch them a step earlier: a stock printing a volume thrust (3x+ its own normal) while its relative strength is rising but still modest (0 to +25) - the moment a quiet stock wakes up, before it's an obvious leader.

What we found when we tested it
Honestly: catching stocks BEFORE strength shows up (buying the quiet pullback) is NO better than random - you'd be front-running the only signal that works. But catching the thrust itself, with RS accelerating, hits target about 1.5x the base rate. That's real, but roughly HALF the edge of a confirmed leader (~3x). So Emerging is an earlier, lower-confidence entry, not a better one.
How to use it
A watch-and-size tool: earlier means more upside if right, but more failures, so size smaller and keep the stop tight. It's the cohort STALLION would have appeared in on its first thrust day, well before the obvious breakout.

RIGHTWAY group mentions

Cross-references your dashboard picks against what your RIGHTWAY Telegram channel has been talking about (last 45 days). The value is CONFLUENCE: when the tool independently flags a strong setup AND the group is also on it, that's a second, unrelated vote. The RW column and the "In RIGHTWAY" filter show it.

Admin vs. members (the "^" and "*")
Most mentions are member chatter (noise). A "^" means the ADMIN - the broadcast channel that posts the actual calls - called it (hover for the date; many are old). A "*" means the admin flagged it important with a conviction tag (POSITIONAL PICK / MULTIBAGGER / LONG TERM / FOCUS) recently. The admin call carries more weight than random member buzz - use the "Admin call" filter to isolate every stock the admin has ever called (with a target or conviction tag).
The admin's target
When the admin posted a target (e.g. "TARGET-260 AND 300+"), expand the row to see it. A recent call shows "admin target Rs X (+Y% from here)" or "(already reached)"; an old call (>6 months) shows "admin's old target Rs X" with the date - because the entry it was based on is long gone, the live upside math would be misleading. Note: most admin calls in this data are 9-24 months old, so treat them as historical context, not live targets. That's the ADMIN'S stated goal, not the tool's - the tool's own volatility-based target/stop are still the ones with a backtest behind them.
Read it honestly
This channel is heavily promotional and often posts winners AFTER they've already moved - so a mention means "they're discussing it," NOT that it's a good buy. About 43 of 60 dashboard names get mentioned, so a plain mention isn't selective; the admin "^"/"*" markers are far more meaningful than raw count. A proper backtest of whether the admin's calls actually PRECEDE moves is the planned next step.
How to use it
Best as a confirming nudge on a stock the tool already likes - especially "strong setup + fresh ADMIN call" - never as a reason to buy something the tool doesn't rate.
